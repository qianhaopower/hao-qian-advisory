A pattern I’ve been thinking about with emotions:

We assume reality comes directly from the information in front of us.

But often, nothing new happens. Only how it feels changes.

You send a message and don’t get a reply soon enough. If you’re calm, it’s easy to see the possibilities — they might be busy or haven’t seen it. But if you’re already a bit anxious, the same silence can start to feel like something is wrong.

A manager says, “Let’s talk about it tomorrow.” It could mean many things, but in the wrong state of mind, it quickly feels like bad news.

Someone walks past you without saying hello. Maybe they didn’t see you. But if you’re already feeling uncertain, it’s easy to take it personally.

In all these cases, nothing actually changed. The situation stayed the same, but it started to feel different. That shift didn’t come from new information. It came from emotion.

Emotion doesn’t just respond to reality — it helps create it. It acts like a reality generator, shaping what feels real in the moment.

To compensate for this:

Pause and separate two things: what is actually happening, and what your emotion is adding to it.

Ask yourself: how much of this is in the situation, and how much is coming from how I feel?

Because emotion is acting as a reality generator — and a new feeling can look like new evidence, even when there isn’t any.
