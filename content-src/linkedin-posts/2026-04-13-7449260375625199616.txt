I’ve been thinking about what actually limits us today.

It’s not information. 
It’s attention.

We now live in a world with near-unlimited information, 
where attention has a limited budget.

Not because of willpower. 
Because of biology.

Our brain can only process so much at once. 
Focus fatigues. Task switching has a cost—like swapping memory between RAM and disk. 
And dopamine keeps pulling us toward novelty, not depth.

So while information keeps expanding, 
our capacity to process it doesn’t.

That creates a tension:

infinite input, 
finite processing power.

The constraint is no longer access. 
It’s allocation.

So the real skill now is simple: structuring how your attention is used.

Three practical ways to do it:

• Separate thinking work from reactive work 
Don’t mix planning, problem-solving, and decision-making 
with emails, messages, and admin tasks.

• Group meetings by context 
Put one-on-ones from the same project on the same day, 
so your brain carries context instead of reloading it.

• Allocate days to different thinking modes 
Forward-looking days for planning and decisions. 
Backward-looking days for reviews and analysis.

Because in an information-rich world, 
attention is the bottleneck that shapes everything else.
