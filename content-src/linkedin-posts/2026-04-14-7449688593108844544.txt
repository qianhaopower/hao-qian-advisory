Most of us assume thinking is one process.

It’s not.

It’s two very different systems working together—similar to the idea in Thinking, Fast and Slow.

I call them The Workshop and The Factory.

⸻

The Workshop is slow, effortful, and logical—this is your conscious mind.

It’s where you:
• Plan and organise (like scheduling or following instructions)
• Think step by step through problems
• Reflect, reason, and create new ideas

It shows up when you’re working something out deliberately, instead of reacting automatically.

But it’s limited. It gets tired. It can only handle a few things at once.

⸻

The Factory, on the other hand, is massive.

It runs in the background, processing far more information than your conscious mind ever could.
It’s where intuition lives.

It’s why:
• A chess master “just knows” the right move
• You instantly trust (or distrust) someone
• Athletes make split-second decisions

But here’s the catch:

👉 The Factory gives you answers
👉 Without explaining how it got them

⸻

So what does this mean for us?
 1.	Your gut isn’t random—it’s trained.
Your intuition is built from everything you’ve seen, felt, and experienced.
 2.	But it’s also biased and incomplete.
If you rely only on instinct, you’re limited to your past.
 3.	Real thinking happens when both systems work together.
• Let the Factory generate fast insights
• Let the Workshop challenge and refine them

⸻

The real shift for me was this:

Instead of asking
“Should I trust my gut or think it through?”

I now ask:
👉 “Which system is speaking right now—and what’s missing?”

For example:

You meet a candidate and instantly think,
👉 “This person feels like a great fit.” (Factory)

That instinct can be useful—but it can also be influenced by things like likability, similarity, or first impressions.

So instead of stopping there, you switch to the Workshop:
• Do their skills actually match what the role needs?
• Can they handle the challenges we know are coming?
• Am I weighing evidence—or just reacting to how they come across?

Then you zoom out again:
👉 Does it still feel right after thinking it through?

⸻

Because better thinking isn’t about choosing one.

It’s about learning when to slow down… and when to trust what’s already been learned.
