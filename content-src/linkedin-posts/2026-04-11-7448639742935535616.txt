A pattern I’ve been thinking about with money:

We don’t perceive money in a linear way.

If you save $20 on a $200 purchase, it feels like a good deal.

Now imagine saving $2,000 on a $20,000 purchase.

That’s 100× more money saved. But it doesn’t feel 100× better.

If we are honest, it might feel 3, 5, maybe 10 times better — but rarely 100.

When the number is small, we think in absolute terms. 
$20 is just $20.

When the number is large, we shift to proportion. 
$2,000 becomes 10% of $20,000.

When we compare them, we naturally use percentage because large numbers are harder to grasp, so the difference feels much smaller than it really is.

To compensate for this:

When the number is large, slow down. 
Force yourself to think in absolute value — what does this actually mean in dollars?

When the number is small, speed up. 
Don’t spend disproportionate time chasing small savings.
