## Script as written (captions follow what he ACTUALLY says)

As a manager, which ball are you going to drop?
Not whether you'll drop one.
Which one.

As the area you manage grows, so does the number of balls:
Dependencies, meetings, decisions, fires, broken services, and people
conflicts.

Early in your career, you can juggle faster and smarter, and somehow
keep everything in the air.
But eventually, that becomes impossible.

So stop asking:
"How can I keep every ball in the air?"
Start asking:
"Which ball can I afford to drop?"

Make sure it's rubber.

A service issue with no immediate customer impact can wait.
A decision that isn't blocking anyone can wait.
Those balls can bounce back.

But some balls are glass:
Customer trust, data safety, your team's respect, and organizational
culture.

Lose customer data, and the damage may be irreversible.
Lose your team's respect, and it may take years to earn it back.

Great leaders don't keep every ball in the air.
They know which ones to protect—and which ones to drop.

So, which ball are you going to drop?
And are you sure it's rubber?
