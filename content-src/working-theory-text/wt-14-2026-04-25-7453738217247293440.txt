Working Theory #14 — The Last Thing Gets the Weight

You’re reviewing someone’s performance.

Most of the year was steady.

Then something happened recently.

---

Not necessarily bigger. 
Just more recent.

---

And almost without noticing,

it starts to carry more weight.

---

You see the same pattern in projects.

Months of consistent progress.

Then a recent delay.

---

Nothing about the timeline changed.

But the emphasis did.

---

This isn’t a mistake.

It’s how memory works.

Recent information is easier to access, 
so it quietly becomes more influential.

---

At work, this shows up in subtle ways:

Performance reviews 
Project narratives 
Post-incident discussions 

---

Not as a bias we don’t understand,

but as a shift we don’t always see happening.

---

A small check helps:

“Am I weighting the full timeline, 
or just what’s most present in my mind?”

---

Because often,

what carries the most weight 
is simply what came last.
