Working Theory #12 — The Reserved Room Theory

A parent says to their child:

“No matter where you go, there will always be a room for you here.”

It’s a simple sentence.

But it means everything.

---

Now imagine a different offer:

“No matter where you go, we’ll give you money for a hotel.”

Clean room. 
Warm food. 
Good service.

---

Technically, it solves the same problem.

But it doesn’t feel the same.

Not even close.

---

Because the room is not just physical space.

It’s cognitive space.

It means:

“I am holding a place for you in my mind.”

No matter where you are. 
No matter what happens.

---

That’s why it feels powerful.

---

And this doesn’t just apply to families.

It applies to any relationship 
that plays a meaningful role in your life.

---

At work, you see it too.

The people you work best with 
are not just the ones you talk to often.

They’re the ones you’ve made space for.

You know:

- what they’re trying to achieve 
- what they’re worried about 
- where they’re under pressure 

---

You’re not just reacting to what they say.

You’re relating to what they’re dealing with.

---

That’s what creates trust.

That’s what creates alignment.

---

Strong relationships are not built 
by more communication alone.

They’re built by how much space 
you reserve for someone in your mind 
even when they’re not in the room.
