Working Theory #7: Juggling Ball Theory

As you grow into leadership, one thing becomes unavoidable:

👉 You will drop balls.

Not because you are careless — 
but because it’s impossible not to.

Your day is full of things to respond to, decide, and push forward. 
Each of them is a “ball” you are holding.

Early on, you can manage them all. 
But over time, the number, weight, and impact all increase.

At some point, you are no longer holding — 
you are juggling.

And once you are juggling, dropping is part of the job.

So the question changes:

👉 Not “How do I keep everything in the air?” 
👉 But “Which balls can I afford to drop?”

Because not all balls are the same.

Rubber balls bounce back:
- A delayed reply 
- A non-critical meeting 
- Tech debt you consciously take on 
- A priority with a flexible deadline 
- A managed risk with a mitigation plan 

These can recover.

Glass balls break:
- Customer data loss or a data breach 
- Company reputation 
- Trust with your team 
- People issues and team dynamics 
- The culture you shape every day 

These are hard — sometimes impossible — to repair.

Great leaders don’t juggle better. 
They decide better.

👉 Drop rubber balls. Keep juggling the glass balls at all cost.

Because leadership is not about control. 
It’s about judgment under constraint.
