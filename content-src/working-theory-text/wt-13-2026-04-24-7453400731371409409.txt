Working Theory #13 — The Urgency Illusion

You’re looking at something online.

You weren’t planning to buy.

Then you see:

“Only 3 left in stock.” 
“Sale ends in 12 minutes.”

Suddenly, it feels there is a push

---

But pause for a second —

what actually changed?

The product didn’t. 
Your need didn’t. 

Only your feeling did.

---

When something looks limited,

your brain switches to:

👉 “act now, or lose it”

---

That instinct made sense once.

When resources were scarce, 
waiting could mean missing out.

---

But today,

the same response gets triggered 
by signals — not reality.

---

And this shows up at work all the time.

---

In a large, complex organisation,

multiple teams are pushing at the same time.

A product launch needs to go out. 
An incident needs attention. 
A stakeholder wants a fast answer. 

Each one sounds urgent. 
Each one has a reason.

---

Deadlines get pulled forward. 
Messages get stronger. 
Everything starts to feel like “now”.

---

Most of the time,

this isn’t manipulation.

It’s people trying to move things forward.

---

But as a leader,

you can’t treat all urgency equally.

---

Some things are truly time-critical.

Many just feel that way 
because of how they’re framed.

---

The real job is to ask:

“Did the situation change, 
or did the signal change?”

---

That question creates space.

Space to think. 
Space to prioritise. 
Space to protect focus.

---

Because not everything that counts down 
actually matters.
