Working Theory #15 — The First Anchor Wins

“How about $1,200?”

“No, that’s too much.”

---

But something has already happened.

---

Even though you said “too much,” 
your mind is already working around that number.

$1,000? 
Maybe $900?

---

You’re no longer thinking:

what should this actually be?

You’re thinking:

how far away from $1,200?

---

That’s the anchoring effect.

---

Experienced negotiators know this.

They don’t react to the first number.

They step back and ask:

what is this actually worth?

---

And this shows up at work too.

---

I’ve seen many technical projects — refactors, redeployments — initially described as “about a week of work.”

But once someone goes into the details, it turns out to be a few hours.

---

The first estimate wasn’t wrong on purpose.

It was just a quick starting point.

---

But if no one challenges it,

the thinking stays at that level.

---

That’s the challenge for a technical manager:

Go into the details. 
Understand what’s actually there. 

Don’t let the first estimate anchor your judgment.

---

Even if you question the estimate, 
you might still be thinking around it.

That’s the anchoring effect.
