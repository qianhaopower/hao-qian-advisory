Working Theory #29 — Learning Depth

Learning is the process of teaching the brain:

“This matters.”

---

Our brains allocate effort based on perceived depth.

A shallow problem gets shallow attention.

A deep problem gets sustained attention.

---

This happens everywhere.

Learning a few chess rules is shallow.

Mastering chess is not.

Because behind the rules are layers:

patterns, 
strategy, 
timing, 
position evaluation, 
psychology.

---

The same thing applies to languages, music, engineering, leadership, and almost anything meaningful.

---

The more depth we see in a subject, 
the more attention we allocate to it.

And repeated attention changes something important:

The brain starts treating that knowledge as valuable enough to keep.

---

That is learning.

---

We repeatedly return to something, 
practice it, 
think about it, 
connect it to other things,

until the brain slowly internalizes it.

---

Attention is not just focus.

Attention is training the brain about what matters.
