Working Theory No.8: Desk Theory — Rotation

In my previous Desk Theory, we talked about the slope.

But in practice, the desk doesn’t just tilt. 
It gets rotated.

Every time someone comes to you with a problem, they’re — often unconsciously — trying to turn the desk slightly toward you.

Not because they’re avoiding responsibility. 
But because it makes sense.

You have more context. 
More experience. 
More authority.

—

Sometimes, they’re right.

I’ve had situations where the stakes were high and the trade-offs unclear — 
and pulling the problem back to my desk was the right move.

But I’ve also had situations where I took it back too quickly — 
and unintentionally made myself the default owner.

—

That’s the difference.

Not whether work flows back — 
but whether it flows back by default.

—

Good management is not about keeping the desk fixed.

It’s about noticing when it’s being turned — 
and making a conscious decision each time.

Let it rotate when it matters. 
Turn it back when it doesn’t.

—

Would you want that?
