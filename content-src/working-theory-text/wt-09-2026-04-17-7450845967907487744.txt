Working Theory #9 — The Room Tension Theory

You’re in a meeting.

A disagreement starts to build.

Product wants speed. 
Risk wants control. 
Engineering wants clarity.

No one says it directly.

But you can feel it.

---

Some people try to solve it.

They push for decisions. 
They argue for a direction.

Sometimes it works.

Sometimes it just makes things tighter.

---

And then there’s someone who does something different.

They don’t rush to resolve.

They pause.

They shift the conversation.

“Feels like we’re pulling in different directions here.” 
“Maybe we’re all trying to protect something important, just in different ways.”

The tone in the room softens.

People stop talking over each other.

You can feel the tension ease, even if just a little.

It’s still there.

But it’s no longer blocking the conversation.

And from that point on, things start to move again.

---

In a simpler world, 
this wasn’t a core skill.

You could just decide and move on.

---

But today, work is more complex.

More teams. 
More perspectives. 
More moving parts.

More urgency. 
More uncertainty. 
More things no one fully understands yet.

---

If connection breaks, 
nothing moves.

---

From my own experience, 
I’ve often seen this ability show up strongly in many female leaders and colleagues.

Not by pushing harder.

But by noticing what’s unsaid.

By sensing where tension is building 
before it becomes visible.

And by bringing people back into alignment 
without forcing the room.

---

And in today’s world,

that ability matters more than ever.
