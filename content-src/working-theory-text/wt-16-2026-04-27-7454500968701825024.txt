Working Theory #16 — The Recovery Budget

“Friday is leg day. I never miss it.” 
“I train every day. Rest is for the weak.”

Consistency gets praised. Pushing harder gets respected.

But there’s a flaw in the logic:

more load → more progress

---

That’s not how it works.

Your body doesn’t improve from training. 
It improves from recovering from training.

And recovery is not unlimited.

---

Training is only part of the load.

There’s also:

- work pressure 
- tight deadlines 
- unexpected issues 
- life outside work 

Your body doesn’t separate them. 
It adds them up.

---

The same is true for teams.

In some teams — especially when leadership experience is still developing — we unconsciously add load at a steady pace:

- more projects 
- more expectations 
- more deadlines 

---

But the team’s recovery capacity isn’t constant.

Some weeks, the load fits. 
Other weeks, it overwhelms.

Not because the work changed, 
but because everything else did.

- stress increases 
- context switching goes up 
- energy drops 

---

The same amount of work no longer costs the same.

---

That’s when quality slips, decisions get rushed, and people burn out.

The goal is not to keep pushing. 
It’s to find the right stretch — one your team can recover from.

Not too easy. Not overwhelming.

---

That’s where growth happens.

Teams don’t grow from pressure alone. 
They grow from pressure they can come back stronger from.
