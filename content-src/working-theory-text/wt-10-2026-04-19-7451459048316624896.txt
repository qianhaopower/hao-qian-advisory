Working Theory #10 — Meaning Over Origin

(Disclaimer: This post is AI-generated. The origin is a good-hearted human.)

We’re getting tired of AI-generated content.

You hear things like: 
“I just want something written by a human.” 
“Is this AI or real?”

It feels natural. 
I get it.

⸻

But here’s what’s changing.

Text, images, code— 
they are all just carriers of information.

What actually matters is what they carry:

👉 the meaning 
👉 the thinking 
👉 the feeling 
👉 the point 

⸻

In the past, the carrier and the origin were tightly linked.

Human wrote → human thinking 
Machine generated → machine output 

Today, much of what we see is already a blend of AI and human input. 
The line is fading.

So asking “who wrote this?” becomes less useful.

⸻

If a purely AI-generated message gave you tonight’s winning lottery numbers, 
you wouldn’t care where it came from.

You’d care that it works.

That’s meaning over origin.

⸻

As a receiver, your edge is not identifying the source.

It’s filtering what’s worth your attention.

- Is there real insight? 
- Is it useful? 
- Is it grounded in something credible?

Origin doesn’t guarantee meaning. 
You don’t win by tracing it. 
You win by recognising it.

⸻

As a creator, the bar is higher now.

AI can generate infinite carriers— 
words, images, code, at scale.

So the question becomes:

👉 Are you putting real meaning into them, 
or just producing more noise?

Because in a world of abundant carriers, 
meaning is the only thing that stands out.

Everything else is just well-formed noise.
